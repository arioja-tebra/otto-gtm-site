Editorial Policy & Source Standards

Practice guidance should make its sources and limits easy to find.

Use named, relevant sources.

Regulatory and billing statements link to government guidance or the documentation of the product being described. Worked examples are labeled hypothetical. We separate a calculator’s output from a market benchmark or prediction.

Show the author and review date.

Articles display an organizational byline, source-check date, and links to supporting material. A named reviewer is added only after that person has completed and approved the review. No expert review is claimed for the current drafts.

Keep product claims specific.

Product pages describe publicly documented workflows. Unverified pricing, security, or availability details stay explicit. We do not treat standalone tools as evidence of integrated EHR capabilities.

Check changes before publication.

Confirm product and competitor descriptions against current documentation. Review legal, coding, and financial guidance with the appropriate owner. Update both the visible page and its structured data when the underlying facts change.

Current collection status.

This is an internal review edition prepared October 16, 2026. The content was drafted with AI assistance using Mateo’s package and the linked sources. Final editorial approval, specialist review, and the author assignment are pending.
