SOAP or DAP? Work through the format.

We compare how each format organizes the same information, so you can choose an approach that fits your practice’s documentation requirements.

The practical difference

SOAP separates Subjective and Objective information. DAP brings those observations and reports together under Data. Both include an Assessment and Plan.

We start with the information you need to document.

A format gives the note a structure. Your clinical setting, documentation policy, payer requirements, and the encounter determine what the record needs to contain. We’d choose the structure that helps you make those distinctions consistently.

InformationSOAPDAP
Client-reported experienceSubjectiveData
Observations, measures, interventions, and responseObjective or the appropriate section under your documentation policyData
Clinical interpretation and progressAssessmentAssessment
Agreed next stepsPlanPlan

Try the same fictional session in both.

Our examples follow a fictional client practicing a response to work messages. In SOAP, we separate what the client reports from what happens in the session. In DAP, those details share a Data section. The assessment still needs the clinician’s reasoning in either format.

Three questions we would use to choose

  1. Does the format meet the documentation requirements that apply to your work?
  2. Can another authorized clinician follow the encounter and the next steps?
  3. Can you distinguish the client’s account, observed information, and your assessment without duplicating whole paragraphs?

There is no universal winning format. Test a fictional encounter in each, review it with the appropriate clinical owner, and use the structure your practice approves.

How this relates to Otto

Otto’s public workflow describes SOAP and DAP drafts from session audio, followed by clinician review and signature. A draft still needs checking against the encounter. Our template examples are editorial illustrations; they are not generated by the Otto product.
